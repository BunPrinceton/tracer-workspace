Goal:

  • Trainees will learn and develop an understanding of the anatomical characteristics and significance of CA3 pyramidal cells and mossy fibers.
  • Trainees will acquire the ability to proofread CA3 pyramidal cells and mossy fiber axons based on the detailed anatomical structures learnt, applying their knowledge in practical scenarios.
  • Guidelines will be provided on the significance of ignoring certain small mergers and the impact of this decision on the overall accuracy and efficiency of the proofreading process.

Parking Lot

Mouse Neuron 101

  • Basic Neuroanatomy: Overview of neuron structure and functions, focusing on the mouse as a model organism.
  • Types of Neurons: Introduction to different neuron types in the hippocampus, their roles, and their importance in brain function.
  • Practical Session:

3. "Teleportation Method" for Navigating Misaligned Spots

  • Concept Introduction: Explain what the "teleportation method" is and its relevance in navigating through digital neuron tracing tools.
  • Identifying Landmarks: Teach how to identify reliable landmarks that help in reorienting when lost or when encountering misalignment in tracing pathways.
  • Hands-On Training: Practical exercises to practice the teleportation method and landmark identification.

4. Process of Elimination Methodology

  • Theory: Discuss the process of elimination as a problem-solving method to decide which continuation is the best option.
  • Exercise: Practical exercises to apply this methodology on a provided location/neuron.

5. Understanding of Glia Morphology

  • Glia Functions and Morphological Identification: Overview of the ultrastructure and the morphology of glial cells in 3D, and brief introduction of their functions in the nervous system. Teach how to differentiate glia from neurons and recognize distinct morphological features of glial cells.

6. Differentiating Excitatory vs. Inhibitory Cell Morphology

  • Cell Type Characteristics: Detailed explanation of the morphological differences between excitatory and inhibitory neurons.
  • Interactive Practice: Use a few practice cells to reinforce learning about how to differentiate these two types.
